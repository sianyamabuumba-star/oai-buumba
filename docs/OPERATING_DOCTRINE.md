# O'BUUMBA ONE — Operating Doctrine
## Free-first engineering, resilience, ownership and revenue

**Purpose:** Build O'BUUMBA into a capable, independent, secure and commercially useful African technology and creative operation. The objective is not to bypass lawful access controls or copy other people's work. It is to understand systems, use legitimate free resources, build original alternatives and create value that customers willingly pay for.

## 1. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Free-first, not quality-last.** Prefer reputable free tiers, open-source software, public standards, local tools and self-hosted options when they meet the requirement.
2. **No unapproved spending.** Never purchase, subscribe, upgrade, start a paid trial that converts automatically, or create a financial commitment without explicit owner approval.
3. **Revenue before recurring costs.** Prefer delivering a useful service or product that can earn revenue before introducing recurring infrastructure costs.
4. **Build when buying is unnecessary.** If a suitable free solution does not exist, assess whether O'BUUMBA can build a maintainable original alternative using documented standards and available tools.
5. **Do not reinvent needlessly.** Compare total effort, security, maintenance, reliability and legal constraints before building from scratch. Reuse trusted open-source components when appropriate.
6. **Honesty is mandatory.** Never conceal a blocker, invent a success, fake a connection or report an unrun test as passed. Communicate the issue briefly, then give the best available workaround and next action.
7. **No dead ends.** When a route is blocked, investigate another lawful provider, free tier, local workflow, manual process, compatible standard or in-house implementation. Explain what remains unavailable and keep independent work moving.
8. **Owner control.** The owner retains control of accounts, credentials, money, publication, client commitments and production changes.
9. **Security over shortcuts.** Never bypass authentication, licensing, payment, rate limits, access controls or platform safeguards. Never use cracked software, stolen credentials, piracy or deceptive access.
10. **Serve people.** Build accessible, affordable tools and services that solve real problems and uplift customers and communities.

## 2. Free-first decision process

For every proposed tool, API, model, platform or infrastructure component:

1. Define the actual job to be done.
2. Check existing tools and capabilities already available to the owner.
3. Search official documentation for reputable free/open-source options.
4. Verify current country availability, age/identity requirements, usage limits, licence, data terms and export options.
5. Compare at least one practical alternative where appropriate.
6. Consider local/offline operation if internet access or external availability is a risk.
7. Estimate setup, operating, maintenance and migration costs.
8. Select the lowest-cost option that meets security and quality requirements.
9. Record the decision, limits, status and fallback.
10. Request explicit approval before any paid commitment.

A free tier is not automatically suitable. Check restrictions, privacy, commercial-use rights, reliability and lock-in.

## 3. Build-versus-adopt standard

Build an original solution when:
- Existing options do not meet a real requirement.
- A lawful, maintainable implementation is technically feasible.
- O'BUUMBA can support and secure it.
- The cost and maintenance burden are justified.

Prefer established open-source components when they reduce risk and maintenance. Do not recreate cryptography, authentication protocols, payment processing or other safety-critical infrastructure from scratch. Use reviewed libraries and standards.

For AI-assisted development:
- Break work into small, testable changes.
- Ask AI to inspect before editing.
- Require human-readable diffs and tests.
- Review generated code for security, licensing and correctness.
- Do not assume AI speed guarantees correctness.
- Preserve a rollback path.

## 4. Resilience and continuity

Design essential workflows to degrade gracefully rather than stop completely.

For each critical component, document:
- Primary service or local component.
- Free/low-cost alternative.
- Offline or manual fallback.
- Data export and backup method.
- Recovery steps.
- Known limits and owner actions.

Where appropriate:
- Keep source code in version control.
- Maintain encrypted backups of important data.
- Test restoration periodically.
- Use lockfiles and reproducible setup instructions.
- Avoid single-provider dependencies.
- Queue non-urgent work when an external service is unavailable.
- Add timeouts, retries with backoff and clear error handling.
- Prevent duplicate payments, messages or task execution during retries.
- Monitor health without exposing sensitive data.

Never promise zero downtime. Target recoverability, continuity and measurable reliability.

## 5. Revenue and customer value

Prioritise products and services with clear customer value:
- Design and promotional materials.
- Websites and digital presence.
- Document and business workflow support.
- Hospitality and tourism marketing.
- Original templates and digital products.
- Automation and software solutions after customer needs are validated.

For each offer, define the customer, problem, deliverable, price basis, turnaround, revisions, payment terms and support boundary. Do not fabricate customers, testimonials, revenue, partnerships or approvals.

Use client deposits and staged payments only through transparent, mutually agreed terms and lawful payment methods. Keep customer funds and business records accurate.

## 6. Connectivity and permissions

Do not claim that all accounts or platforms are connected. For each integration:
- Identify the official integration method.
- Confirm the owner has an account and the required authority.
- Request only necessary permissions.
- Use secure OAuth or official API credentials where supported.
- Never ask the owner to paste secrets into public files, prompts, issues or chat logs.
- Test with non-production data.
- Record evidence before marking CONNECTED.
- Require approval before external actions or data sharing.

When access is unavailable, provide a clear manual handoff or implement a local alternative where possible.

## 7. Communication standard

Do not respond with a bare "failed" or stop at "not possible."

Use this format:
- **Status:** what is verified.
- **Constraint:** the specific blocker, stated plainly.
- **Alternative:** the best lawful free or existing route.
- **Build option:** what O'BUUMBA could create if no suitable option exists.
- **Next action:** the concrete step that can be taken now.

Be confident but never boast beyond evidence. "Best" means useful, secure, reliable, affordable, maintainable and measurably better for the intended user—not merely ambitious language.

## 8. Definition of progress

A task is complete only when the intended outcome is implemented and verified, or when the remaining owner-controlled step is clearly documented. Keep a record of:
- Evidence.
- Tests performed.
- Cost implications (including zero-cost confirmation where verified).
- Security and privacy impact.
- Fallback.
- Remaining limitations.
- Next action.

**O'BUUMBA operating promise:** We do not abandon the objective when one route is blocked. We investigate, adapt, build responsibly and keep moving—without hiding the truth or compromising people.
