# O'BUUMBA Universal Product & Service Lifecycle

## Purpose
This lifecycle is the shared operating method for every O'BUUMBA offering: client services, physical products, digital products, software and SaaS, creative intellectual property, consulting, AI evaluation work and internal/community initiatives.

The record schema is `schemas/offering.schema.json`. Business units and revenue tracks are catalogued in `registry/business-units.json`.

## Lifecycle stages and exit evidence

| Stage | Required work | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity | Capture the problem, source, audience and initial idea | Opportunity record and owner |
| Research | Study public market evidence, alternatives, users and constraints | Dated source list and research summary |
| Validation | Test demand, feasibility, risk, rights, cost and eligibility | Decision with evidence and assumptions |
| Definition | Define audience, value, scope, deliverables and exclusions | Approved offer brief |
| Planning | Tasks, timeline, budget assumptions, tools and dependencies | Plan and assigned responsibilities |
| Development | Create the service method, product, code or creative work | Versioned working artefact |
| Quality assurance | Test accuracy, usability, accessibility, security, originality and rights | Review checklist and test evidence |
| Launch ready | Prepare packaging, instructions, pricing approval and support arrangements | Launch-readiness sign-off |
| Active | Make the offering available and maintain its records | Published/available status verified |
| Marketing | Prepare and distribute approved campaigns | Drafts, approval and publication evidence |
| Sales | Manage enquiry, qualification, quote, acceptance and order | Customer decision and order record |
| Fulfilment | Deliver agreed work or product and handle revisions | Delivery and acceptance evidence |
| Support | Handle issues, questions, maintenance and feedback | Support history and resolution |
| Improvement | Review quality, costs, demand, conversion and feedback | Measured review and next action |
| Growth | Expand, renew, bundle or serve new audiences responsibly | Evidence-backed growth decision |
| Retirement | Stop sales, notify affected parties where required, preserve records and support obligations | Closure checklist and reason |
| Retired | No longer offered; retained only as required | Archived record and retention decision |

A product can remain active after its launch project is complete. Project completion is not product retirement.

## Stakeholder distinction
Record separately where relevant:
- Creator or owner
- User
- Beneficiary
- Buyer
- Payer
- Approver

These roles may be held by different people or organisations. Do not assume the user is the buyer or the payer.

## Commercial and delivery controls
- Draft prices and cost assumptions are not verified financial records.
- A quote is not an accepted order.
- An order is not proof of payment.
- Payment status requires appropriate evidence.
- Production completion is not delivery confirmation.
- Delivery is not customer acceptance unless the agreed process says so.
- Track refunds, disputes, revisions, support and recurring obligations separately.

## Rights, privacy and governance
Before launch, record asset provenance, licences, AI tool terms, third-party materials, client permissions and human contributions. Do not assume generated output is exclusive company IP. Do not use client-confidential material in third-party AI tools without checking provider terms and obtaining required authorisation.

Legal entity status, ownership and signing authority must be verified before issuing contracts or invoices in a company's name. Where age or account eligibility rules apply, never bypass them; an eligible authorised adult must handle restricted steps where required.

## Shared lifecycle rules
1. Every offering has a unique ID, business unit, owner, stage, next action and history.
2. Stage changes require the relevant exit evidence.
3. Blocked items record the blocker, responsible person and review date.
4. External publication, messaging, spending, purchases, contracts, account changes and sensitive data sharing require appropriate approval.
5. Retired offerings retain necessary support, legal, tax and recordkeeping obligations.
6. Review the lifecycle template periodically using actual operational feedback.
